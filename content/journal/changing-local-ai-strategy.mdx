## Initial Approach

My initial goal was absolute privacy and zero-latency execution. I wanted my entire automation and scripting pipeline to be powered by local models running via Ollama. I spent weeks configuring systemd services, writing Python wrappers, and trying to squeeze performance out of sub-billion parameter models.

## What Felt Wrong

While the latency was fantastic, the intelligence just wasn't there. Small models were great for summarization, but they hallucinated JSON structures and lost context during multi-turn automation commands. I found myself writing more Regex to fix the LLM's output than I was writing actual business logic. It became clear that local models on consumer hardware are currently better suited as assistants than orchestrators.

## What Was Discovered

I realized I was treating LLMs as deterministic functions. I needed to separate the "brain" (which requires heavy reasoning) from the "nervous system" (which requires speed and structure).

## What Changed

I shifted to a hybrid architecture:
1. **Local/Deterministic:** Basic intents, wake-words, and strict API routing are handled locally using standard programming logic and very small embedded models for vector search.
2. **Cloud/Reasoning:** Complex, ambiguous queries are offloaded to cloud APIs (like Gemini or OpenAI) where context windows and reasoning capabilities are significantly higher.

## Why The Architecture Evolved

This evolution taught me a crucial lesson in system design: don't force a technology to solve a problem it isn't ready for. By offloading the heavy lifting, my system's reliability increased tenfold, and I can still retain privacy by only sending sanitized, necessary context to the cloud.
