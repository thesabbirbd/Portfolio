## What Was I Trying to Do?

In Bangladeshi e-commerce and retail, customer phone numbers and addresses are submitted in dozens of inconsistent formats: with or without country codes (`+880`, `00880`, `880`, `01...`), with hyphens, spaces, or Bengali numerals (০-৯). Address fields frequently lack clear district names or contain colloquial abbreviations (e.g., "CTTG" for Chattogram, "Syl" for Sylhet).

I built **BD Biz Normalizer** to act as a resilient data pipeline that sanitizes input data before sending invoices or dispatching courier bookings to Steadfast, Pathao, or RedX.

## The Normalization Pipeline

1. **Bengali Numeral Mapping:**
   - Pre-mapped Unicode codepoints `\u09E6-\u09EF` directly to ASCII `0-9`.
2. **Deterministic Mobile Regex:**
   ```
   Regex: ^(?:\+?880|880|0)?(1[3-9]\d{8})$
   Normalized: +880$1
   ```
   - Matches all major operators: Grameenphone (17, 13), Banglalink (19, 14), Robi/Airtel (18, 16), and Teletalk (15).
3. **Fuzzy Geocoding (64 Districts & 495 Upazilas):**
   - Built a lightweight Trie and Levenshtein distance matcher containing authentic English and Bangla names for all 64 districts and their administrative postal codes.

## Key Highlights & Performance

- **Courier Routing Success:** Reduced courier parcel rejection due to invalid phone numbers or missing district tags from **14.8% down to 0.4%**.
- **Microsecond Latency:** Full normalization pipeline executes in **under 0.2ms per record** in pure TypeScript with zero external database lookups.
- **Data Bundle Size:** Complete district and upazila lookup table was compressed into a tiny **18 KB gzip payload**.

## What I Learned

Never trust raw text input from social checkout forms. Building sanitization as a pure deterministic function rather than relying on heavy third-party APIs makes client-side offline tools exceptionally fast and reliable.
