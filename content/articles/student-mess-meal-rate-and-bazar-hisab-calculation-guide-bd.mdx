## Bachelor Mess Management in Bangladesh

Living in a student or bachelor mess in Dhaka, Rajshahi, Chattogram, or Sylhet is a defining milestone for millions of university students and young job seekers. However, managing the monthly mess accounts—especially the daily bazar deposits, fluctuating meal counts, and common utility bills—often leads to interpersonal friction if calculations are done on messy paper sheets.

## The Core Meal Rate Formula

At its foundation, the mess meal rate follows an elegant mathematical principle:

```
Meal Rate = Total Net Bazar Expenditure (৳) ÷ Total Consumed Meals Across All Members
```

### Step 1: Net Bazar Accounting
Every morning or evening, the appointed manager records the exact bazar cost. At the end of the 30-day billing cycle, all daily bazar slips are summed into the gross bazar total.

### Step 2: Total Meal Counting
Each member registers their daily meals:
- **Breakfast:** Typically counted as 0.5 meal or managed individually.
- **Lunch & Dinner:** Counted as 1.0 meal each.
- **Guest Meals:** Added directly to the host member's total meal count.

### Step 3: Fixed Common Utility Costs
Fixed overheads—including the cook's wage (*Khala bill*), cylinder gas refill, WiFi internet router subscription, and house cleaning items—must never be mixed into the per-meal rate. These are divided equally among all active residents:

```
Common Share Per Member = Total Utility Overheads (৳) ÷ Total Active Members
```

## Individual Member Balance Reconciliation

For each individual member, their final balance is determined by comparing their deposited cash with their actual total mess liability:

```
Total Liability = (Meals × Meal Rate) + Common Share
Final Balance = Cash Deposit - Total Liability
```

- If Final Balance is positive: The member is in surplus and will receive a refund (*ফেরত পাবে*).
- If Final Balance is negative: The member has a deficit and must pay the remaining balance (*দিতে হবে*).
